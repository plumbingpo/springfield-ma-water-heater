How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Water at the tap that is suddenly scalding hot
  • A complete loss of hot water throughout the house
  • Loud popping, banging, or rumbling from the tank that has gotten worse quickly
  • Standing water around the water heater that returns after you wipe it up
  • Water or steam coming from the temperature and pressure relief valve or its discharge pipe
  • A rotten-egg smell near a gas water heater (leave first, and call the gas utility)
  • Black soot or heat discoloration around the burner door or draft hood
  • A breaker that trips every time the electric heater tries to run

Tanks rarely fail all at once. They corrode from the inside for months, and the warning signs show up long before the floor gets wet. If you notice any of these signs, contact Springfield Water Heater at (413) 200-3427 for a prompt inspection.

Why Hire a Professional for Emergency Water Heater Repair?

A water heater emergency stacks several hazards in one small space. There is water on the floor near a 240-volt circuit on electric models, or near a gas valve, burner, and flue on gas models. There is a tank holding water at high temperature and household pressure. And there is a safety valve that, if it is discharging, is doing so for a reason. Homeowners sometimes try to stop the relief valve by capping it, crank on a leaking fitting until it snaps, or relight a pilot beside a gas smell, and each of those can turn a bad afternoon into a real disaster. The safest moves for a homeowner are the simple ones: leave for gas, then shut off water and energy, then call for help.

Professional help is not just about having wrenches. It is about knowing why the failure happened so it does not happen again next month. When a relief valve discharges, we measure static water pressure and check whether an expansion tank is missing or waterlogged. When a tank floods the floor, we locate the true source, whether it is the valve, a fitting, a connector, or the tank wall. When a gas heater dies, we check thermocouple or flame sensor signal, gas pressure, and venting before restarting it. The diagnosis determines everything that follows. Components can be replaced the same visit; a failed tank cannot be patched, and you hear that straight and explain the replacement path.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Keep the thermostat near 120F to limit scalding and reduce strain on the tank
  • Store chemicals and flammables far from the burner, and keep the area around the heater uncluttered
  • Ask about a drain pan and drain line, especially for heaters in attics, closets, or above finished rooms
  • Tag the water heater supply valve and the main shutoff so nobody has to search during a leak
  • Test the T&P valve annually; a valve that sticks or weeps afterward should be replaced
  • Flush sediment from the tank yearly, since buildup overheats the bottom and speeds corrosion
  • Consider an automatic leak shutoff that stops the cold supply the moment water hits the floor
