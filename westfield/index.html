Keeping Your Westfield Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year in hard-water areas.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as discolored hot water, popping and rumbling, dampness under the tank, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.

Hot Water Headaches Westfield Homeowners Face

Long before a tank floods a Westfield garage, it usually sends signals. Left alone, they end in a failed tank and water on the floor. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it needs a professional look soon. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
